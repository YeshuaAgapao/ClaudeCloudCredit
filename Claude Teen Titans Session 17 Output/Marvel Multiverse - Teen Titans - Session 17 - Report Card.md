## How to Read This

### The grading scale

| Grade | Meaning |
| :---: | :---- |
| **SSS** | Legendary. A campaign-defining moment. |
| **SS** | Extraordinary. The best thing anyone did all session, by a distance. |
| **S** | Exceptional. Clearly beyond what the sheet and the situation asked for. |
| **A** | Excellent. |
| **B** | Good. Above expectations. |
| **C** | Adequate. Did what was needed, and no more. |
| **D** | Below expectations. Clear mistakes, or capability left on the table. |
| **E** | Poor. Mistakes that cost the team. |
| **F** | Failing. It went wrong, or it didn't happen when it was needed. |
| **G** | Harmful. Worse than doing nothing. |

`+` and `−` refine grades A through E. **—** means not applicable. Nobody earned SSS, SS or G in Session 17.

### Ground rules

* **Session 17 only.** Each grade measures what the character did this session against what his sheet and the situation made possible. A **C** means a Rank 3.5 Titan with that sheet did what was needed. Career standing lives in the Rankings & Analysis document.
* **Decisions versus dice.** Most sub-aspects grade decisions. *Damage Output* and *Evasiveness & Survivability* grade outcomes, dice included. Each card's **Dice context** line says where the dice decided things.
* **Section grades are holistic, not averages.** They start from the spread of sub-aspect grades and move by up to one step for whatever actually decided the session. A high mark for restraint in a quiet category does not lift a section on its own.
* **The Overall grade leans non-combat, roughly 65 / 35.** Session 17 was a 24-hour hold, a maze and a long conversation, with one bonus round and one full round of combat at the end.
* **Out-of-character knowledge is used freely**, as in the Rankings document. **⟨Speculative⟩** marks a reading the log does not confirm, and **⟨Ruling⟩** cites a house rule.

**The session in one paragraph.** Pulled out of the Session 16 aftermath into a pocket dimension, the Titans woke in a military bunk room and quickly worked out three things. They were in another dimension. They were under hidden cameras, with dormant power nullifiers in the walls. They would be returned to the exact moment and place they had left. They spent the 24-hour hold resting, eating replicator burgers and arguing over whether to practise a formation, and never built one. Quasar-Force earned a one-minute penalty for pulling a nullifier off the wall, and it froze him at the starting door; the team spent most of that minute arguing in the doorway. Then they ran the maze cleanly (flight, a keypad solved in seconds, two group teleports, and a trap that Intrepid and Lock-Step solved together) and reached the colosseum first. There they met a Titans team from another Earth that seemed more interested in talking than fighting. They learned from the announcer that the two teams could set their own length and win conditions, above a 30-second floor, and then let the other side choose every matchup. The fight opened well: Edge one-shot Four-Fold, and Quasar-Force threw the healer who revived her out of the arena. By the end of Round 1, Oracle had been stunned by his own copied parry, Edge was stunned, Intrepid was on his back, Quasar-Force was restrained and Lock-Step was frozen by a penalty. **The team won the information war and lost the planning one, and the fight is roughly even at the pause.**
